<ReadingSection title="Topic focus">

Managers must understand how decisions in one area affect **all parts of the business**. The key functions are **interdependent** — they must work together so products are produced and delivered consistently.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="The four key business functions">

| Function | Role |
|----------|------|
| **Operations** | Improve production processes; factory or office layout; transform inputs into outputs |
| **Marketing** | Identify target markets; pricing, product features, promotion and distribution |
| **Finance** | Fund activities; budget allocation; financial record keeping |
| **Human resources** | Recruit, train, contracts and separation of employees |

Each function completes different tasks, but **all must align** to achieve business goals.

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="Interdependence definitions">

- **Interdependence** — business functions rely on each other; a change in one affects others  
- **Operations management** — activities managers engage in to produce goods or services  
- **Marketing** — planning and executing product, pricing, promotion and distribution to satisfy customer needs  
- **Human resource management (HRM)** — effective management of the formal employer–employee relationship  

</WorkbookSection>

<ReadingSection title="Why interdependence matters">

Examples of coordination:

- Marketing research shapes **operations** (what to produce) and **HR** (skills needed)  
- **Finance** sets budgets that constrain marketing spend and staffing  
- **Operations** quality affects marketing claims and customer satisfaction  
- **HR** training supports operations efficiency and marketing service standards  

Managers who understand interdependence make decisions that support the **whole business**, not just one department.
