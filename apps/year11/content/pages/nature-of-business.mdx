<ReadingSection title="Role of business — producing goods and services">

Businesses combine resources to create products that satisfy customer needs and wants.

**Goods** are tangible items; **services** are tasks performed for customers by others.

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="Key definitions">

- **Business** — the organised effort of individuals to produce and sell, for a profit, products that satisfy needs and wants  
- **Goods** — items that can be seen or touched  
- **Services** — things done for you by others  
- **Finished product** — ready for customers to buy and use  
- **Production** — activities that combine resources to create products  

</WorkbookSection>

<ReadingSection title="Contributions of business to society">

| Contribution | Summary |
|--------------|---------|
| **Profit** | Revenue minus expenses; reward for risk and effort |
| **Employment** | Jobs and income so consumers can purchase goods and services |
| **Incomes** | Wages, salaries, dividends for individuals and shareholders |
| **Choice** | Competition gives consumers variety and competitive prices |
| **Innovation** | New or improved products, services and processes |
| **Entrepreneurship & risk** | New ventures explore untapped markets; risk must be managed |
| **Wealth** | Assets minus debt; often linked to property and investment |
| **Quality of life** | Material and non-material wellbeing from employment and ethical business practice |

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="Income and risk definitions">

- **Income** — money received for labour, or business return on investment  
- **Wage** — payment for work, often hourly or daily  
- **Salary** — fixed regular payment to permanent employees  
- **Shareholders** — part-owners of a company  
- **Dividend** — share of company profits paid to shareholders  
- **Entrepreneurship** — creating business wealth through vision and coordinated inputs  
- **Risk** — possibility of loss or failure; must be identified and managed  

</WorkbookSection>

<ReadingSection title="Types of businesses">

Businesses can be classified by **size**, **geographical reach**, **industry sector** and **legal structure**.

**Size (ABS):**

- Micro — fewer than 5 employees  
- Small — 5–19 employees  
- Medium — 20–199 employees  
- Large — 200+ employees  

**SMEs** — fewer than 200 FTE employees and/or less than $10 million turnover; ~98% of Australian businesses.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Business life cycle">

Businesses pass through stages: **establishment → growth → maturity → post-maturity** (renewal, steady state, or decline).

<LessonImage src="/images/diagrams/business-life-cycle.svg" alt="Business life cycle from establishment through growth and maturity to post-maturity" caption="The business life cycle — strategies differ at each stage" variant="diagram" />

<LessonImage src="/images/curated/business-lifecycle-table.png" alt="Table summarising challenges and strategies for each stage of the business life cycle" caption="Challenges and strategies at each stage — see also downloadable PDF on Resources page" variant="diagram" />

Each stage brings different challenges and strategies. Owners must assess their position on the cycle to choose appropriate responses.

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="Cessation and liquidation">

- **Voluntary / involuntary cessation** — including bankruptcy and **liquidation**  
- **Liquidation** — liquidator sells assets to pay creditors  
- **Insolvent** — unable to pay debts as they fall due  
- **Voluntary administration** — administrator attempts to trade out of difficulty  

</WorkbookSection>
