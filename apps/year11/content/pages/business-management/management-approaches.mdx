## Planning, Organising, Controlling

Classical Approach - Scientific The classical scientific approach to management was developed by Frederick Taylor who based the production processes on time and motion studies to discover HOW to produce things efficiently and effectively. Overall, he wanted to systematise the way the work was done. He used time and motion studies that showed how long each task took in the production process. He trained poorly skilled workers to complete repetitive tasks and created division of labour so tasks were divided into small, specialised activities . He believed employees only worked for their own self interest and would only work if tightly supervised . This required many layers of management to ensure workers were supervised to be completing the work allocated to them. Based on scientific principles to achieve the 'one best way' of doing things Example: Henry Ford and automated car assembly Classical Approach - Bureaucratic Developed by Weber and Fayol and shifted the focus from the production process to the business organisation . Bureaucracy refers to a set of rules and regulations that control a business He believed that there should be a strict hierarchal structure with clear lines of communication and responsibility (line management). This led to processes that need to be followed with rules and regulations in place to allow everyone know what their role in the business is. His most important principles of management stem from the ideas that: Discipline is a key feature of leadership An organisation should come before personal interests Rewards for effort should be fair Security of employment is essential and teamwork should be encouraged. Due to the long chain of command , there is a very NARROW/ SHORT span of control that each employee has within the business. That is s/he will only be in control of the small area of the business they work in. They will not have access to other parts of the business or necessarily see where their role fits into the whole picture. Classical Leadership Style - AUTHORITARIAN/AUTOCRATIC

### Characteristics

Management make all the decisions and dictate work methods Very close supervision of workers ‘do it my way or go home’ Very little employee input

## Summary Of Classical Management

Behavioural Management Approach More attention needed to the human aspect of the organisation Worker participation in production process was essential to make productivity gains. Founded by Elton Mayo who conducted experimental studies and discovered the ‘Hawthorne Effect’, which exemplifies the idea that by meeting the social needs of workers has a significant effect on productivity.

## Leading, Motivating, Communicating

Management functions under the behavioural approach changed from being a controller to more of a facilitator, and the functions of management are: Leading - this is the process of influencing or motivating people to work for the achievement of the organization. A strong leader has trust in their employees, will delegate work and leads by example. Motivating – Understanding of employee’s needs and motivations is important in wanting them to complete work. A customized balance between monetary and non-monetary rewards need to be considered. Millennials are mostly motivated by intrinsic rewards and will stay in an organization ONLY if they feel they are making a difference. Communicating – Open and transparent communication must come from all levels within the organisation. Managers must be sensitive to cultural differences and how non-verbal ques impact their employees. Short chain of command encouraging 2 way communication and a wider span of control . Less layers of management Focus on team approach Self directed work teams Management asks employees for their suggestions when making decisions and considers the input when making decisions.

### Therefore

Decision making authority is shared Contributions are welcomed and valued = raises levels of commitment and motivation More flexible and adaptable to change.

## Summary Of Behavioural Management

Contingency Management Approach More contemporary approach to management. Recognises & stresses need for flexibility and adaptability to management. Each situation is unique and should be treated that way. Managers should therefore borrow and blend aspects of each theory. Under the Organising approach will depend on what is going on at the time and can change from being a controller to facilitator depending on circumstance. Therefore the functions include: Motivating Communicating Planning Organising Controlling Negotiating Principles The importance of flexibility and adaptability to changing circumstances.
