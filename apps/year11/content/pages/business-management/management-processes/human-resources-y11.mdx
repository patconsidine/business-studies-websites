<ReadingSection title="Topic focus">

Employees can **make or break** a business. **Human resource management (HRM)** is the effective management of the formal relationship between employer and employees.

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="HR cycle overview">

| Stage | Focus |
|-------|--------|
| **1. Acquisition** | Attracting and recruiting the right staff |
| **2. Development** | Training and preparing employees for greater responsibility |
| **3. Maintenance** | Retaining employees — morale, productivity, reducing turnover |
| **4. Separation** | Ending the employment relationship — voluntary or involuntary |

<ReadingSection title="Stage 1 — Acquisition">

**Recruitment** — seeking applicants and selecting the most suitable candidate.

| Method | When used |
|--------|-----------|
| **Internal** | Appoint someone already in the business |
| **External** | New position, resignation, retirement — seek outside applicants |

**Job analysis** — systematic study of duties, tasks and work environment.

**Case study — McDonald's Snaplications (2017):** applicants applied via a **Snapchat lens** in uniform and sent a 10-second video to @Maccas — engaging a younger demographic.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Employment contracts and standards">

An **employment contract** is a legally binding agreement — either party may take legal action if terms are broken.

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Modern award** | Minimum standards for everyone in an industry |
| **Enterprise agreement** | Agreement between employer and a group of employees in a specific business |
| **Common law contract** | Agreement between employer and one employee |

**National Employment Standards (NES)** — 10 minimum conditions legislated in 2010 for full-time and part-time employees (generally not casuals).

**Awards** set minimum wages and conditions; some businesses pay **above award** to attract and retain staff.

**Enterprise agreements** must include: nominal expiry date, dispute procedures, individual flexibility arrangements, consultation on major workplace change.

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="Employment terms">

- **Modern award** — legally binding minimum wages and conditions for an industry group  
- **Enterprise agreement** — collective workplace agreement between employer and employees (often via union)  
- **National Employment Standards (NES)** — 10 minimum employment conditions under the Fair Work Act  
- **Unfair dismissal** — dismissal that is harsh, unjust or unreasonable — may be heard by the Fair Work Commission  

</WorkbookSection>

<ActivitySection title="Employment contracts — workbook questions">

1. Define **employment contract**. What are the obligations of employers and employees?  
2. Which employees benefit from the 10 NES?  
3. Select the **three most important** NES and justify.  
4. Define **award** — recall four matters in modern awards.  
5. Distinguish awards from enterprise agreements.  
6. Extension: would you prefer an award or enterprise agreement? Justify.

</ActivitySection>

<ReadingSection title="Stage 2 — Development">

**Training** — teaching staff to perform their job more efficiently (knowledge and skills).

**Development** — preparing employees for **more responsibility** through knowledge, skills and experience.

**Aim:** long-term change in skills, knowledge, attitudes and behaviour to improve performance. Lack of training/development hurts competitiveness and increases turnover.

| Type | Example |
|------|---------|
| **Formal off-the-job** | External conference or course |
| **Informal on-the-job** | Learning a new POS system in a retail shop |
| **Competency-based** | Identifying skill gaps and targeting training (e.g. medical education) |

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Stage 3 — Maintenance">

Concerned with **retaining** employees — supports productivity, high morale and lower turnover costs. (Explored in more depth in Year 12 HSC.)

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Stage 4 — Separation">

Ending the employment relationship — **voluntary** or **involuntary**. Restructuring has increased **redundancies**.

**Fair Work Act 2009** led to: Fair Work Commission, modernised awards, unfair dismissal laws for larger businesses, good faith bargaining, National Employment Standards.

**Case study — unfair dismissal (2016):** a cleaner fired for having coffee before his shift won reinstatement and damages — dismissal found unjust and unreasonable.

</ReadingSection>
