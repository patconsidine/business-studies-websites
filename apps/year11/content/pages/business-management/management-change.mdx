<ReadingSection title="Topic focus">

Businesses must **respond to internal and external influences** and manage **organisational change** effectively — or risk falling behind competitors.

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="Key syllabus terms">

- **Change** — any alteration in the internal and/or external environments  
- **Organisational change** — adoption of a new idea or behaviour, changing how the organisation operates over time  
- **Transformational change** — major restructuring (culture, technology, employee structure)  
- **Incremental change** — small adjustments to procedures that build over time (e.g. paperless office)  
- **Outsourcing** — contracting outside businesses to complete tasks  
- **Strategic alliance** — two or more businesses team up for a venture or mutual benefit  

</WorkbookSection>

<ReadingSection title="What drives change?">

Change may stem from:

- Changing **consumer tastes**  
- New **production methods**  
- Shifts in **markets or products**  
- Changes in how **employees** perform tasks  

Organisational change affects **culture**, **structure**, **skills** required and **work practices**. Effective change management helps a business **succeed**; poor management can lead to failure.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Internal and external influences">

| Internal | External |
|----------|----------|
| Management — e.g. hierarchical → **flat** structures | **Competition** — local, national, global |
| Employees — work-life balance, desire for **involvement** | **Legislation** — e.g. cigarette/vape rules, COVID measures |
| | **Social** — changing consumer tastes |
| | **Technology** — online-only business models |

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Managing change effectively">

Managers should be **proactive**, not only reactive. Successful change management uses:

- Sound **leadership** skills  
- **Responsive** organisational structures  
- Clear communication  

Change involves **risk** — but managed well, it can be productive.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Identifying the need for change">

Take a **holistic view** of the external world and potential impacts on the business.

**Business information systems (BIS)** gather, organise and summarise data into practical information for managers — e.g. tracking **absenteeism** after a new process is introduced.

Goals for change must be **SMART** and guided by the **vision statement**. New goals must be **achievable** — attainable and realistic.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Case study — PlayStation">

Sony engineer **Ken Kutaragi** saw an opportunity in gaming after being disappointed by Nintendo's console quality. He consulted for Nintendo while at Sony, then convinced Sony's CEO to enter the market — creating the **PlayStation**, one of the greatest product innovations in history. Example of **intrapreneurship** and managed strategic change.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Types of change">

| Type | Examples |
|------|----------|
| **Transformational** | Restructuring; new culture; e-commerce adoption; technology based on cost–benefit analysis |
| **Incremental** | Small procedure changes; long-term shift to paperless office |

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Structural responses to change">

**Outsourcing** — contracting external businesses for non-core tasks (call centres, cleaning, security). Cost-effective but has risks — e.g. governance scandals when outsourced advisers breach confidentiality.

**Flatter structures and work teams** — delegate to front-line staff; shorter chain of command, wider span of control → better communication, productivity and profitability.

**Strategic alliances** — businesses team up for a venture; networking where each member supplies what others cannot fully provide.

**Example:** Qantas Frequent Flyer partnership with Woolworths — loyalty points boost across both brands.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Resistance to change">

Some employees find change hard to accept. Implement change through **regular communication** between employers and employees.

| Reason for resistance | Examples |
|----------------------|----------|
| **Financial costs** | New equipment; David Jones $275m store upgrade |
| **Staffing** | Redundancy payouts; retraining time and cost; low morale; **inertia** (complacency) |

**Driving forces** support change; **restraining forces** work against it.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Reducing resistance">

Strategies include:

- Creating a **culture of change**  
- Using **change agents** — influential people who help manage transition  
- **Effective communication**  
- **Positive leadership** — employees accept risk when managers show concern for wellbeing  

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Management consultants">

A **management consultant** has specialised knowledge — often in **change management**, a methodical approach at business and individual levels.

Businesses hire consultants for:

- Wide **business experience**  
- Specialised knowledge and skills  
- **Objective external** viewpoint  
- Latest **research** and industry **best practices**  

Role: investigate problems and develop **plans for improvement**.

</ReadingSection>
