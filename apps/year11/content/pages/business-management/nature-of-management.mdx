Management Skills - Reconciling the conflicting interests of stakeholders Recall a stakeholder is a person that has a vested interest in the business. The main aim is to manage the interest of the various stakeholders within a business. Stakeholders can be external (society, environment and customers) and internal (employees, managers and shareholders). Contemporary businesses must ensure that the triple bottom line is being considered when the strategic position of the business is being considered.

### Strategies to overcome conflict include

Increasingly society is demanding that businesses accept responsibility for their actions. E.g. comply with law, be ecologically sustainable in their operations etc. In a desire to reconcile conflicts between various stakeholders, some businesses use the process of stakeholder engagement. This refers to businesses sharing information with and seeking input from stakeholders, and involving them in decision-making. Achieving Business Goals A goal is a desired outcome (target) that an individual or business intends to achieve within a certain time frame. Reasons for business goals Businesses develop goals as they provide a business with a framework to work by. Therefore they: Serve as a target Serve as a benchmark Increase motivation and commitment of employees when targets are reached. Using the S.M.A.R.T. technique — a common acronym in goal setting — is the best method for writing effective goals. Using this technique will help the business owner to define the goals more easily, and the details involved in achieving them. S.M.A.R.T. goals should be: Specific - Goals should be straightforward and emphasise what the business wants to happen. Measurable - Decide on goals whose progress can be measured so the business owner can see the change occur. This helps the business stay on track. Achievable - Goals need to be challenging but not be too far out of the business’s reach, otherwise the business owner and employees will become unmotivated due to the lack of success. Realistic - The goals must represent something that the business owner and employees are both willing and able to work towards. Timebound - The goals must have deadlines and sub-deadlines attached to them, otherwise the commitment is too vague.

### Main business goals include

Financial Goals Profits Profit maximization occurs when there is a maximum difference between total revenue and total costs Usually achieved as a long-term goal Short term goals may be to reduce price or gain profit from marketing campaign Market Share Market share refers to the business’s share of the total industry sales Gained through the development of Product range Different brands and branding techniques Growth Growth can be achieved internally and externally Internal growth comes through hiring more staff, opening more business fronts and introducing more products and gaining more sales from innovation External growth comes through mergers and acquisitions of businesses Growth for SMEs Smaller growth focus Share Price Only applies to Public Companies High share price for shareholders equates to more dividends Profit should be steadily increasing but should not be achieved unethically Social Goals Community – supporting a local event or cause Provision of Employment – creating jobs in the area your business operates Social Justice – ensures all people within and affected by the business are treated equally Environmental Goals All businesses must engage in sustainable developments An ecological sustainable development is one that meets the needs of current people without jeopardizing the ability for future generations to meet theirs. Achieving a mix of business goals Businesses do not generally have only one specific goal; they have a range of goals because they have different stakeholders who each have different needs. Managers therefore have a mix of goals that they try to achieve simultaneously. Goals are interdependent so, by focusing on achieving a mix of goals, the overall success of the business is more likely. Strategies implemented to achieve a goal such as increased market share will support improved profitability. So too will human resources strategies that improve the skills and productivity of employees. Social and ecological goals will support a business’s reputation and therefore its marketing goal of increased sales. This mix of goals reflects the interdependence between the key business functions.

### Staff involvement is achieved through

Motivation Mentoring Innovation Training Motivation Motivation refers to the individual, internal process that directs, energises and sustains a person’s behaviour. Makes people want to work

### Motivation is usually developed through

The use of either rewards or punishments to motivate employees. For generations managers have believed that money is the most powerful motivating force. However, recent research tends to suggest that employee motivation depends on a wide range of factors besides money. Non-financial rewards can have an even more substantial impact on employee satisfaction and motivation than traditional financial rewards. Motivational techniques that consider the emotional and physical wellbeing of employees lead to high morale, which in turn improves employee and business effectiveness.
