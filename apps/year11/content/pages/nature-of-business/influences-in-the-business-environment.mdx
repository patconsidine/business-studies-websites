<ReadingSection title="Topic focus">

No business operates in **isolation**. Every decision about what to produce and how to operate is shaped by the **environment** in which the business operates.

Successful managers understand this environment — they respond positively to change and prepare for its impact on operations.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="External vs internal influences">

| | External | Internal |
|---|----------|----------|
| **Control** | Factors the business has **little control** over | Factors the business can **identify and adjust** |
| **Examples** | Economy, law, technology, competition | Product mix, location, resources, culture |
| **Response** | Monitor and adapt strategy | Feedback, evaluation and direct management decisions |

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="External influence — economic">

**Economic influences** relate to economic activity at country, regional or global level:

- Interest rates, wages, unemployment, exchange rates, inflation  

Economic forces affect a business's **capacity to compete** and customers' **willingness and ability to spend**.

| Phase | Characteristics |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Boom** | Expansion — increased spending, production and employment |
| **Recession** | Contraction — decreased spending, production and employment |

When economic problems appear, consumers become **more cautious** and confidence falls.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="External influence — financial">

Businesses access **global finance** more easily today. **Debt finance** is heavily influenced by **interest rates**:

- Rates **rise** → businesses cautious about new debt  
- Rates **fall** → businesses more willing to borrow  

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="External influence — geographic">

**Geographic influences** — climate, natural resources, topography, infrastructure and **location**.

Australia's position in the **Asia–Pacific** and growth in nations such as **China** create opportunities for expansion, sales and profit — but also competitive pressure.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="External influence — social">

**Societal attitudes** — ideas, values and beliefs held in a society — shape **behaviour** (how people live, work and consume).

Rapid response to changes in tastes, fashions and culture can create **growth opportunities**. Failure to respond can threaten **stability and viability**.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="External influence — legal">

Businesses face increasing **legal obligations**. Laws may require a business to stop or start certain activities. Non-compliance can mean **fines or imprisonment**.

Compliance is often **time-consuming and costly** — regulations can be confusing or contradictory.

**Example:** NSW Public Health orders during **COVID-19** forced supermarkets to introduce purchase limits, social distancing and customer caps in stores.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="External influence — political">

**Political change** creates uncertainty or confidence. With regular elections at all levels, politics affects most major business issues.

**Example:** debate around phasing out **live sheep exports** from Australia ([agriculture.gov.au](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/export/controlled-goods/live-animals/livestock/live-sheep-exports-phase-out)).

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="External influence — institutional">

Three main **institutional influences:**

- **Government**  
- **Regulatory bodies**  
- **Other groups** — trade unions, employer associations  

**Example:** the **Fair Work Commission** can set minimum wage increases affecting business costs ([ABC News, 2023](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2023-06-02/minimum-wage-increased-by-5-75-per-cent-2023/102426044)).

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="External influence — technological">

**Technology** — tools, techniques and systems used to solve problems or serve a purpose in operations.

Innovation has revolutionised workplaces and daily life. Appropriate technology can:

- Increase **efficiency and productivity**  
- Create **new products**  
- Improve **quality and range** of goods and services  

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="External influence — competitive situation">

**Competition** to be market leader or win customer loyalty can benefit consumers (choice, quality, price) and businesses (efficiency, innovation).

Each business seeks a **sustainable competitive advantage** — strategies that maintain an edge over rivals for a long period.

**Ease of entry** depends on **market concentration:**

| Market type | Ease of entry |
|-------------|---------------|
| **Perfect / monopolistic competition** | Many small firms — entry relatively easy |
| **Oligopoly** | Few large firms — entry difficult; significant resources needed |
| **Monopoly** | One dominant firm — new competitors cannot enter |

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="External influence definitions">

- **External environment** — factors outside the business over which it has little control  
- **Sustainable competitive advantage** — long-term edge over competitors through strategy  
- **Ease of entry** — how easily a new business can establish itself in an industry  
- **Market concentration** — how many firms dominate an industry  

</WorkbookSection>

<ReadingSection title="Case study — ALDI in Australia">

German chain **ALDI** (founded 1946) entered Australia in **2001** with stores at Bankstown and Marrickville, Sydney. Strategy: **home brands**, low prices, minimal advertising — word of mouth.

By 2019: **500+ stores**, 13 000+ employees, significant GDP contribution, **12.1%** grocery market share (2018). Named Australia's **most trusted brand** (Roy Morgan, 2018).

ALDI's entry drove **price deflation** — Coles and Woolworths rethought their models. Savings benefited all grocery buyers, not only ALDI shoppers.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Markets — financial, labour, consumer">

Changes across market types affect businesses:

| Market | Change | Business impact |
|--------|--------|-----------------|
| **Financial** | Easier access to overseas funding | More capital options; currency risk |
| **Labour** | Increased demand for skilled workers | Recruitment and wage pressure |
| **Consumer** | Global reach via e-commerce | Larger potential customer base |

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Internal influences">

Internal factors can be adjusted through **feedback and evaluation**:

| Influence | How it affects operations |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Product** | Type of goods/services — e.g. café menu changes through the day; fresh food needs regular deliveries |
| **Location** | Visibility, costs, proximity to suppliers, customers and support services |
| **Resources** | Who to hire; information needed (advice, reports); physical inputs; finance sources |
| **Management** | Roles required; staffing levels; cross-training decisions |
| **Business culture** | Shared values, ideas, expectations and beliefs — "the way we do things here" |

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="Internal influence definitions">

- **Internal environment** — factors within the business that management can influence  
- **Business (corporate) culture** — values, ideas, expectations and beliefs shared by staff and managers  
- **Product mix** — range of goods and services offered  

</WorkbookSection>
