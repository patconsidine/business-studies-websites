<ReadingSection title="Small to medium enterprises (SMEs)">

SMEs are the backbone of the Australian economy — roughly **98%** of businesses are SMEs (fewer than 200 FTE employees and/or less than **$10 million** turnover).

At June 2021 there were **2.4 million** actively trading businesses. Entry and exit rates fluctuate with the **business cycle** — SME numbers tend to rise in a **boom** and fall in a **recession**.

| Indicator | Role of SMEs |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Employment** | Major source of jobs, especially in local communities |
| **Innovation** | Often more agile; fill gaps larger firms overlook |
| **Competition** | Keep prices and service quality competitive |
| **Economic contribution** | Drive growth in goods, services and exports |

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="SME definitions">

- **SME (small to medium enterprise)** — fewer than 200 FTE employees; small businesses often defined as ≤ $10 million turnover  
- **Business cycle** — recurring pattern of boom, downturn and recovery in the economy  
- **Entry rate** — new businesses starting in a period  
- **Exit rate** — businesses ceasing operations in a period  

</WorkbookSection>

<ReadingSection title="Influences in establishing an SME">

Before starting, entrepreneurs weigh **internal** factors they can control and **external** factors they cannot.

| Influence | Examples |
|-----------|----------|
| **Personal qualities** | Qualifications, skills, motivation, entrepreneurship, cultural background, gender |
| **Sources of information** | Textbooks, mentors, industry reports, government advice |
| **The business idea** | Gap in the market, competition, feasibility |
| **Establishment options** | New venture, buy existing business, franchise |
| **Market** | Goods/services, price point, location |
| **Finance** | Source and cost of capital |
| **Legal** | Business name, zoning, health regulations |
| **Human resources** | Skills needed, wage and non-wage costs |
| **Taxation** | Federal/state taxes, local rates and charges |

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="The role of a business plan">

A business plan is the **road map** for future growth. It sets goals and direction — without it, owners may not know where the business is heading.

**Common mistake:** preparing a plan once and never referring back to it. **Another mistake:** sticking too rigidly to a plan when conditions change. Plans must be **living documents** — reviewed and adapted.

A comprehensive plan also helps when **arranging finance** — lenders need evidence the business is organised and viable.

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="Business plan definitions">

- **Business plan** — a written statement of the business's goals and the steps needed to achieve them  
- **Executive summary** — concise overview of the whole plan for lenders or investors  
- **Situational (SWOT) analysis** — identification of internal **strengths/weaknesses** and external **opportunities/threats**  
- **Vision statement** — what the business aspires to become in the future  
- **Objective** — a specific, measurable target needed to achieve the vision  

</WorkbookSection>

<ReadingSection title="Common elements of a business plan">

| Section | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| Executive summary | Snapshot for stakeholders |
| The business idea | Description, outlook, legal structure |
| Management and ownership | Who runs the business |
| SWOT analysis | Internal and external assessment |
| Vision, goals and objectives | Direction and measurable targets |
| Operational plans | Production, processes, inputs |
| Marketing plans | Customers, promotion, pricing, place |
| Financial plans | Forecasts, budgets, break-even |
| Human resource plans | Staffing, skills, training |
| Future prospects | Growth and expansion plans |

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="The business idea">

The **business idea** section addresses:

- How the business fills a **gap in the marketplace**  
- **Business name** and branding (e.g. logo)  
- **Prime function** — key goods and/or services  
- **Location** and **legal structure** (sole trader, partnership, company)  

Entrepreneurs must consider the **external** environment (competitors, economy, regulation) and what they can shape in the **internal** environment (culture, resources, processes).

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Vision statements and business goals">

A **vision statement** states what the business aspires to become. It should be concise, creative, focused and realistic — guiding owners, managers and employees and shaping culture.

**Example — McDonald's (2017):** *"To move with velocity to drive profitable growth and become an even better McDonald's serving more customers delicious food each day around the world."*

An **objective** is a specific statement of what must be achieved to reach the vision, usually within a set timeframe.

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="Types of goals">

- **Strategic goals** — long-term, broad aims for the whole business  
- **Tactical objectives** — mid-term, departmental actions supporting strategy  
- **Operational objectives** — short-term tasks supporting tactical and strategic goals  

**Common business goals:** increase market share, market growth, profit maximisation, social goals (CSR), environmental commitment, long-term growth.

</WorkbookSection>

<ReadingSection title="Financial, social and environmental objectives">

| Type | Examples |
|------|----------|
| **Financial** | Market share, growth, profit maximisation |
| **Social** | Community service, employment, social justice |
| **Environmental** | Sustainable development, reduced waste in production |

**Long-term growth** depends on a **sustainable competitive advantage** — e.g. unique product, consistent marketing, relationship marketing for customer loyalty (Apple is a global example).

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Case study: Bondi Sands">

In **2012**, Blair James and Shaun Wilson spotted a gap — no affordable **Australian self-tanning** brand despite strong beach culture and the **solarium ban**.

They developed salon-quality products at an accessible price, pitched to **Priceline**, and used **social media** for brand awareness. By **2018** they were among Australia's largest beauty exporters; **2019** brought a major **Walgreens** (US) deal and expansion into Europe.

**Point of difference:** affordable, **Australian-made** products. **Growth strategy:** international expansion and new product categories (sun care, skincare).

Use this case when explaining **vision → goals → objectives**, **market gap**, and **SME global expansion**.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Organising resources">

An **organisational structure** is the framework showing how tasks are divided, resources used and departments coordinated.

Benefits of effective organising:

- Establishes **chain of command** and communication channels  
- Coordinates departments toward a **common purpose**  
- Allocates resources so employees can perform their roles  

Each key business function needs a plan section in the overall business plan.

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="Organising and finance definitions">

- **Organisational structure** — framework defining tasks, resources and coordination  
- **Equity financing** — giving part-ownership in exchange for funding  
- **Debt finance** — funds obtained through loans  
- **Operations** — transforming inputs (materials, labour, equipment) into outputs  

</WorkbookSection>

<ReadingSection title="Plans for key business functions">

**Operations** — What equipment and materials? Which suppliers? Storage and delivery? Staff expertise?

**Marketing** — Customer needs, promotion, pricing; all staff contribute to satisfying customers while meeting business goals.

**Finance** — Most critical question: *What is the most appropriate source of financing?* Common sources: personal savings, family/friends, bank loans, equity investors, **government grants** (usually for expansion, R&D, innovation or exporting — not start-ups).

**Human resources** — Organisational chart, staffing requirements, qualifications, training (TAFE, tertiary, specialist), and management skills (communication, strategic thinking, problem-solving, adaptability).

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Forecasting">

**Forecasting** predicts future performance to support planning. Consider:

- Set-up and **operating costs**  
- **Sales forecast** — expected revenue in the first 12 months (price × quantity)  
- **Break-even analysis** — sales level where total revenue equals total costs  
- **Cash flow projection** — monthly inflows and outflows for the first year  
- **Budgeted balance sheet** — financial position at year end  

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="Cost and revenue formulas">

- **Total revenue (TR)** = Price (P) × Quantity (Q)  
- **Fixed costs (FC)** — do not vary with output (e.g. rent)  
- **Variable costs (VC)** — vary with output (e.g. raw materials, electricity)  
- **Total cost (TC)** = FC + VC  
- **Break-even quantity** = FC ÷ (P − VC per unit)  

At break-even, **neither profit nor loss** is made. Sales above break-even → profit; below → loss.

</WorkbookSection>

<ReadingSection title="Break-even example — Better Rackets">

<LessonImage src="/images/diagrams/break-even-graph.svg" alt="Break-even graph showing total revenue and total cost lines intersecting at the break-even point with profit and loss zones" caption="Break-even point — where total revenue equals total cost (fixed + variable)" variant="diagram" />

Better Rackets plans a new tennis racquet at **$200** each. Fixed costs are **$600 000**; variable cost is **$80** per racquet.

Break-even quantity = 600 000 ÷ (200 − 80) = **5 000 units**.

Management uses this to judge whether projected sales will generate profit.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Cash flow projections">

A **cash flow projection** shows expected **cash inflows and outflows** over an accounting period (usually 12 months) from operating, investing and financing activities.

It helps the owner estimate the **bank balance** each month and identify **cash shortfalls** — a vital tool for knowing how much capital the venture requires.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Monitoring and evaluating">

**Monitoring** compares actual performance against planned performance:

1. What does the business want to achieve? (performance standard)  
2. Are goals being achieved? (compare actual vs planned)  

Use **budgets**, sales statistics and cost analyses. Focus areas: **sales**, **budget** and **profit**.

**Evaluation** assesses whether stated goals were met and identifies weak spots for improvement.

**Profit** matters because it is: a reward for risk, a goal (maximisation), a source of reinvestment finance, a performance indicator, and (for companies) a basis for **dividends**.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Corrective action">

When standards are unrealistic or the environment changes, **corrective action** is needed — **modifying** the plan using updated information.

Changes may affect materials, delivery, staffing, or responses to internal/external factors. Any part of the business plan may need revision.

</ReadingSection>
