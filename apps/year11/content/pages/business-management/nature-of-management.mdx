<ReadingSection title="Skills of management">

Managers need varied skills to lead different people and tasks. A **skill** is the ability — from knowledge, practice and talent — to do something well.

| Skill area | Purpose |
|------------|---------|
| **Communication** | Convey the right information in the correct way so tasks meet business goals |
| **Interpersonal** | Work effectively with diverse employees and stakeholders |
| **Strategic thinking** | See the big picture and plan ahead |
| **Organisational** | Coordinate resources, time and priorities |

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Traditional vs contemporary management">

| | Traditional | Contemporary |
|---|-------------|--------------|
| **Focus** | Coordinating resources to achieve goals | Working with people in a changing environment |
| **Environment** | Relatively stable | Dynamic — constant adaptation |
| **Emphasis** | Efficiency of processes | Effective and efficient use of people and resources |

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="Management definitions">

- **Manager** — someone who coordinates the business's limited resources to achieve its goals  
- **Goal** — a desired outcome (target) to be achieved within a certain timeframe  
- **Stakeholder** — a person or group with a vested interest in the business  
- **Triple bottom line** — economic, social and environmental performance considered together  

</WorkbookSection>

<ReadingSection title="Reconciling stakeholder interests">

Stakeholders may be **internal** (employees, managers, shareholders) or **external** (customers, society, environment).

Managers must balance **conflicting interests**. Strategies include:

- Accepting **social responsibility** — comply with law, operate sustainably  
- **Stakeholder engagement** — sharing information, seeking input, involving stakeholders in decisions  

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Achieving business goals">

Goals provide a **framework** to work by. They:

- Serve as a **target** and **benchmark**  
- Increase **motivation and commitment** when achieved  

Use **S.M.A.R.T.** goals:

| Letter | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **S** | Specific — clear about what should happen |
| **M** | Measurable — progress can be tracked |
| **A** | Achievable — challenging but reachable |
| **R** | Realistic — willing and able to work towards it |
| **T** | Timebound — deadlines and sub-deadlines |

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Types of business goals">

**Financial goals**

- **Profit maximisation** — maximum difference between total revenue and total costs (usually long-term)  
- **Market share** — the business's share of total industry sales  
- **Growth** — internal (staff, outlets, products) or external (mergers, acquisitions)  
- **Share price** — applies to public companies; affects dividends  

**Social goals** — community support, employment, social justice  

**Environmental goals** — sustainable development; meeting present needs without compromising future generations  

Businesses hold a **mix of interdependent goals** — e.g. HR strategies that improve productivity support profitability; social goals support reputation and marketing.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Staff involvement">

**Staff involvement** includes employees in decision-making and gives them skills and rewards — increasing **labour productivity** (output per employee in a set period).

Policies recognising employee knowledge, skills and creativity lead to long-term success. Achieved through:

| Practice | Summary |
|----------|---------|
| **Motivation** | Internal process directing and sustaining behaviour — not only money; wellbeing and non-financial rewards matter |
| **Mentoring** | Experienced staff guide less experienced employees on attitudes, culture and expectations |
| **Training** | Boost knowledge and skills; **multiskilling** supports team-based work |
| **Innovation** | **Intrapreneurs** solve problems creatively; reward ideas, avoid micromanagement, allow safe failure |

</ReadingSection>
