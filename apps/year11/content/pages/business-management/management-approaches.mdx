<ReadingSection title="Topic focus">

The **management approach** adopted by a business shapes how tasks are allocated, how the organisation is structured, management styles and employee relations.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Overview of approaches">

| Approach | Era / focus | Key idea |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **Classical — scientific** | Industrial revolution, factory production | One best way; efficiency through time and motion studies |
| **Classical — bureaucratic** | Organisation structure | Rules, hierarchy, clear roles |
| **Behavioural** | Human relations | Worker participation and motivation drive productivity |
| **Contingency** | Contemporary | No single best way — adapt to each situation |

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Classical — scientific (Taylor)">

During the industrial revolution, **high demand** drove **factory production** — mass-producing goods efficiently.

Frederick **Taylor** used **time and motion studies** to find the most efficient way to complete tasks:

- Divide work into small, **specialised** activities  
- Train poorly skilled workers for **repetitive** tasks  
- **Tight supervision** — workers assumed to act in self-interest  
- Many layers of management to monitor work  

**Example:** Henry Ford and the automated car assembly line.

**Leadership style:** **Authoritarian / autocratic** — management decides everything, close supervision, little employee input.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Classical — bureaucratic (Weber & Fayol)">

Shifts focus from production to **organisation**. **Bureaucracy** = rules and regulations controlling the business.

- Strict **hierarchical** structure with clear lines of communication  
- **Long chain of command** → **narrow span of control**  
- Employees see only their small area, not the whole picture  

**Fayol's principles** include: discipline in leadership, organisation before personal interests, fair rewards, job security, teamwork.

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="Classical approach summary">

- **Scientific management** — systematise work for maximum efficiency (Taylor)  
- **Bureaucratic management** — formal hierarchy, rules and defined roles (Weber, Fayol)  
- **Autocratic leadership** — decisions from the top; minimal employee participation  

</WorkbookSection>

<ReadingSection title="Behavioural approach (Mayo)">

More attention to the **human aspect**. Worker participation essential for productivity gains.

Elton **Mayo** — **Hawthorne Effect**: meeting workers' **social needs** significantly affects productivity.

Management shifts from **controller** to **facilitator**:

| Function | Behavioural emphasis |
|----------|---------------------|
| **Leading** | Influencing and motivating through trust and example |
| **Motivating** | Understanding needs; balance monetary and non-monetary rewards; millennials often value **intrinsic** rewards |
| **Communicating** | Open, transparent; sensitive to culture and non-verbal cues |

**Structure:** shorter chain of command, **wider span of control**, team approach, self-directed work teams, shared decision-making — raises commitment and adaptability.

</ReadingSection>

<ReadingSection title="Contingency approach">

Contemporary approach — **no single best way**. Each situation is unique; managers **blend** aspects of classical and behavioural theory as circumstances require.

Functions may include: planning, organising, leading, controlling, motivating, communicating and **negotiating**.

**Principle:** flexibility and adaptability to changing circumstances — a manager may be controller or facilitator depending on the situation.

</ReadingSection>

<WorkbookSection title="Compare the approaches">

| | Classical | Behavioural | Contingency |
|---|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| **View of workers** | Need supervision | Motivated by participation | Depends on context |
| **Structure** | Tall hierarchy | Flatter, teams | Flexible |
| **Decision-making** | Top-down | Shared input | Situational |
| **Best for** | Repetitive production | People-centred roles | Dynamic environments |

</WorkbookSection>
