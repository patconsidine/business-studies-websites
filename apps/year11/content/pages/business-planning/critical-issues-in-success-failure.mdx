What leads to an SME's success or failure? importance of a business plan Importance of a business plan It is important for business owners to take the time to develop a business plan - some may be very detailed while others may be less formal. Despite the formal format, a plan should contain the following: a clear, concise statement of the goals of the business — what the business wants to achieve well-developed plans for achieving these goals — how the business is going to achieve the goals reliable control standards for measuring performance — has the business achieved the goals; if not, what needs to be changed? The business plan is the ‘blueprint’ for future growth and development within a business → set out the goals and direction of the business. Management - staffing & teams Management - Staffing & Teams The most important accomplishments a skillful manager will achieve for a business include the ability to: solve difficult problems set and help attain important goals create a positive business culture through their words and actions monitor the business’s external and internal environments coordinate teams and departments monitor the performance of teams and individuals Staffing In order to succeed, a business needs to have employees who are motivated and satisfied as they will be more productive. ### Employee skills databases

A skills audit is a process that establishes the current skills levels of employees and future skills requirements.

### The aim of the audit is to identify

the skills that will give the business a competitive advantage weaknesses in the skills base which, unless overcome by training or recruitment, will threaten the business’s survival. A skills inventory is a database containing information on the skills, abilities and qualifications of existing staff. It is used to search for appropriate candidates to fill new positions within the business. Trend Analysis Trend analysis is a process of investigating changes over time and looking for a pattern (trend) in order to predict the future. Trend analysis is a powerful tool which, when used properly, will assist SME owners to achieve business success by helping forecast such factors as: Potential sales Total revenue Total operating costs Gross and net profits Availability of labour Overall, trend analysis will provide more reliable information on which a SME owner can base decisions. Identifying and sustaining a competitive advantage Competitive advantage refers to the strategies used by a business to gain an ‘edge’ over its competitors. It is very difficult for businesses to achieve and maintain above average performance in all areas of the workplace. As a result, it must choose a strategy (outlined blow) that will develop the strengths of the business: Differentiation strategy Price/cost strategy Ensuring long-term success Differentiation strategy This strategy is where the business offers customers something that is not already offered by business rivals, could be high product quality, innovative design, positive brand image or top-quality service. Once a business achieves its product differentiation strategy, the challenge then is to sustain this competitive advantage, especially as competitors attempt to match this strategy. Price/cost strategy This strategy means that the business leads the way by achieving the lowest production costs, which allows them to offer the product price. It is important that quality is not compromised based on a the business's market position. The main ways to achieve a cost advantage over competitors is to: Improve efficiency of operations: ability of a business to streamline the processes that occur within that business. Achieve economies of scale: the business tries to reduce the cost of inputs by using more machinery, buying in bulk or increasing the specialisation of labour or reducing the cost of labour through outsourcing. These will increase the level of outputs and reduce the cost per unit of production. Ensuring long-term success Long-term success can be difficult to achieve if a business is unable to develop a sustained competitive advantage. To limit the advances of competitors, a business must protect itself and reduce opportunities available to the competitors. This may be achieved through: research and development, which helps the business develop innovative products patents/copyrights, which protect against copying of inventions exclusive contracts with suppliers → supplier gives preference to the business lobbying government to limit foreign competition, which aims to restrict imported products For a business to sustain a competitive advantage, the actions of management must be dynamic → requires constant action and management needs to be able to predict changes and trends and act on these (be proactive). Avoiding Over-extension of Finances Businesses should start small and gradually expand. Some owners overextend their capacity in the establishment or growth phases, which leads to financial strain.

### A business can overextend financially by

using hire purchase and/or leasing commitments made on: Cars Equipment Office furniture Plant and machinery The premises Purchasing excess stock Employing too many staff for the businesses current needs There are many tips for a business of any size and type to avoid overextending financially, including:

### Business planning

this involves the completion of cash flow projections, preliminary establishment costs, personal financial budgets and financial statements of position, along with the setting of the business’s goals and objectives. Avoiding an overdependence on debt financing : this helps avoid problems during weak economic periods when lenders or creditors are unwilling to wait for the business to overcome difficulties. Start small : many businesses ‘walk before they crawl’ and quickly find themselves in difficulty. It is important to start slowly and allow the market to determine the right times for expansion and growth. Overextension of Other Resources Businesses can also overextend on their resources. The following two factors have the potential for problems if they are not monitored by management:

### Stock

A business that overextends in terms of stock has invested too much money in goods or raw materials. May occur if the business anticipates customer demand incorrectly, or purchases a ‘bargain’ from a supplier without establishing whether it is a saleable among its own customers. Stock is unrealised sales so a business who has too much is tying up their own cash. Inventory management systems assist management to ensure they don’t have excessive stock.

### Staff

a business may find itself with too many staff → may be due to over-optimism about sales levels or just poor job allocation and hiring. Staff are costly, but options are available for a business to decrease its dependence on this resource and reduce costs in the long term, through: Outsourcing, redefining current job descriptions or using more equipment/machinery Using Technology Technology has had significant effects on businesses, impacting both the internal and external environments. The changes have made the workplace more adaptable and increased efficiency.

### Advances in information technology include

iPhones, blackberry smart phones, skype and email. These have reduced communication time delays and improved distance communication between colleagues, suppliers and customers. Robotics, machinery and equipment for production processes have allowed for faster production times, higher productivity and greater flexibility → reduces overall costs. E-business E-business (electronic business) is using the internet to conduct business.

### It occurs when a business

uses the internet to find information — such as suppliers, prices and product details — and to keep track of delivery schedules uses email to communicate with customers and suppliers uses the internet for online working and paying of accounts uses its website to market its goods and services E-business can bring a range of competitive advantages to the small business owner, such as:​ E-commerce It refers to the buying and selling of goods and services via the internet — it is a part of e-business. E-commerce can enable a business to grow exponentially as the business is able to reach a significantly greater market through an online store. E-commerce has enabled many businesses to become successful without having the significant expense of a physical presence (rent, local charges associated with a store etc).
